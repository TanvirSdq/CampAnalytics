Operational Guidelines Usage Documentation

1 · Retention Analytics

The Retention Analytics interface evaluates participant continuity between successive campaign editions and cross-event contributor migration:

  1. Campaign Selection: Select one or more configured Wiki Loves campaigns (such as Monuments, Earth, Folklore, Africa, or Bangla). Query codes follow the standard format [event][country][year] (e.g. wlmbd24, wlede22).
  2. Geographic & Temporal Parameters: Select a target country, regional cluster, or all available countries, and define the longitudinal year span. Target query codes are automatically synchronized.
  3. Quick Presets: Pre-configured comparison vectors are available in the presets panel for common evaluation pairs.
  4. Analytical Projection: Choose the desired output format:
    • Data Table: Tabular breakdown of cohort population, intersecting participant count, and retention rate.
    • Heatmap Matrix: Color-coded matrix displaying pairwise retention shares across consecutive editions.
    • Choropleth World Map: Spatial distribution of country-level average or median retention metrics.

2 · Campaign Health Evaluation

The Health Evaluation module computes a multi-dimensional health index (0–100) relative to contemporary regional peer performance:

  1. Target Campaign Code: Provide the standardized identifier for the evaluated edition (for example, wlmbd24 or wlede22).
  2. Baseline Reference: Designate either the automated previous-year edition or specify a custom comparison code. Both target and baseline cohorts must contain valid participant data.
  3. Geographic Standardization Framework: Assign the appropriate regional cluster (e.g., South Asia, Northern & Western Europe, ESEAP). Dynamic benchmark norms are calculated from the top regional performers in that cluster.
  4. Evaluation Output: Review the weighted composite scorecard (0–100), categorical star ratings, quantitative cohort footprints, and automated diagnostic insights.

3 · Year-over-Year New User Influx

The Influx Analytics module assesses continuous community renewal and long-term mobilization:

  1. Series Selection: Choose a campaign type (or All Campaigns), country, and consecutive year span (e.g. {{ DEFAULT_START_YEAR }}–{{ CURRENT_YEAR }}).
  2. Newcomer Detection: Differentiates first-time participants (\(I_t\)) from returning veterans (\(R_t\)) across the series.
  3. Trend Visualizations: Reviews stacked volume bars, net expansion growth rates (\(\Delta \text{YoY}\)), and cumulative community pool milestones.
Analytical Formulations & Calibration Standards Methodological Rigor

Directional Longitudinal Retention

Directional retention from a baseline campaign cohort \(A\) to a subsequent target cohort \(B\) is defined as the proportion of unique contributors in cohort \(A\) who also submitted media in cohort \(B\):

Retention(A → B) = (|U_A ∩ U_B| / |U_A|) × 100%

Where \(U_A\) and \(U_B\) denote the discrete sets of verified upload accounts in each respective category. Retention is non-symmetric: \(Retention(A \to B) \neq Retention(B \to A)\) when total cohort sizes differ.

Year-over-Year Newcomer Influx & Retention

For edition \(C_t\) in a chronological series, newcomer influx isolates contributors not seen in any prior recorded edition \(U_{<t}\):

Influx(C_t) = |U_t \setminus U_{<t}|, \quad \text{Newcomer Share} = \left(\frac{Influx(C_t)}{|U_t|}\right) \times 100\%

Multi-Campaign Ecosystem Influx & De-duplication

When analyzing all campaigns together (all[country][year]), participant sets across all active Wiki Loves photography contests in edition year \(t\) are aggregated into a unified ecosystem cohort \(U_t\):

U_t = \bigcup_{e \in E} U_{e, t}, \quad I_t = U_t \setminus \bigcup_{j < t} U_j, \quad R_t = U_t \cap \bigcup_{j < t} U_j

Where \(E = \{\text{WLM}, \text{WLE}, \text{WLF}, \text{WLA}, \text{WLB}\}\). Contributor cross-participation across contests is de-duplicated, measuring the total net new individuals brought into the Wikimedia movement annually.

Growth Capacity (New Contributor Acquisition)

Growth capacity measures the proportion of target cohort \(B\) participants who are newly acquired contributors with no recorded uploads in baseline cohort \(A\):

Growth(A → B) = (|U_B \setminus U_A| / |U_B|) × 100%

Health Evaluation Weighting Model

The composite health score is computed on a 0–100 scale across five dimensions, each normalized against the upper-quartile performance of peer campaigns in the same geographic cluster. Weights reflect the relative predictive power of each dimension for long-term community sustainability:

  • Retention Index (32%): Proportion of prior-edition contributors who return in the target edition. The strongest single predictor of whether a campaign community survives across years rather than requiring constant re-acquisition.
  • Content Utility (25%): Share of uploaded files actively deployed across Wikimedia projects (prop=globalusage). This is the Wikimedia-specific mission proxy — files that get used in articles deliver encyclopaedic value; files that don't are production without impact.
  • Growth Capacity (18%): Share of the target cohort comprising first-time participants. Growth matters, but is weighted below retention and utility because a campaign that recruits heavily but retains nobody is structurally fragile.
  • Contributor Diversity (15%): Concentration of upload activity among the top 10% of uploaders (lower concentration = higher score). Raised to reflect its role as a leading fragility indicator — campaigns dominated by a handful of power users are vulnerable to collapse when those users disengage.
  • Quality Recognition (10%): Share of submissions formally recognized under Commons quality standards (Category:Quality images, Category:Featured pictures). Kept intentionally modest because QI/FP rates are noisy at small upload volumes and tend to favour niche campaigns over large-participation ones.

Regional Peer Normalization

Rather than applying static global thresholds, the system dynamically identifies active peer campaigns in the same geographic cluster, computes the upper quartile performance envelope, and normalizes target metrics against that cluster standard. This ensures equitable evaluation across mature and developing campaign ecosystems.

Data Pipeline & Infrastructure

Data is extracted in real time directly from Wikimedia Commons. The primary ingestion pathway queries Toolforge replica tools (ptools.toolforge.org) for low-latency category membership enumeration. In case of network interruption or rate limits, requests automatically failover to the official Wikimedia Commons Action API (commons.wikimedia.org/w/api.php) utilizing the categorymembers generator with automatic pagination and exponential backoff.

Analytical Scope & Limitations

  • Evaluations are based on observable on-wiki upload records and do not account for offline activities, workshops, or uncredited organizing contributions.
  • Comparative health scores are inherently relative to the designated regional peer cluster and baseline edition.
  • Incomplete or unheld campaign categories are identified as missing data and excluded from evaluation to prevent erroneous zero-scoring.
  • Content utility and quality rates are calculated from verified category membership and Commons image metadata.
